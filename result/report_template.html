Rating Distributions Lab

Language models are asked to rate words and sentences on the same scales human participants used. Instead of sampling an answer, the probability of every scale point is read straight off the logits — so each item yields a full distribution, comparable to the distribution of the humans who rated it.

01Before you read any number

Three things decide whether a row on this page means anything. They are not caveats bolted on at the end; each one has already overturned a result during this work.

Check mass first

How much probability sat on the valid answers before renormalising. Below ~0.9 the distribution is a rescaled tail: not “the model disagrees with people” but “we never read the model’s answer”. Four apparently sensible results on this page were killed by this check.

The prompt is the instruction, as written

Every model sees the same text the raters saw, with the stimulus in double quotes and nothing appended. A model that does not then answer with a digit shows up as low mass and is flagged, rather than being re-prompted; small Llama models are the usual case.

bfloat16 is fine for rankings, not for single items

Against float32, Pearson moves ≤0.001 and W1 ≤0.005, but 15–40% of items have two scale points at exactly equal probability, because bf16 quantises logits in steps of ~0.031.

Chosen to sit at four different levels of linguistic analysis, and to differ in how much the human raters agree with each other — the quantity that decides how much a distributional comparison can add over a mean.

07Is the measurement itself sound?

Reading probabilities off logits is only worth anything if the reading is correct. Two independent checks, in pipeline/verify_scoring.py.

Internal — padding and positions

In float32, scoring an item alone and scoring it inside a padded batch agree to 3.3e-05. A structural error in the left-padding, the attention mask, or the “read the last position” assumption could not survive that.

External — does the model emit what we claim?

Greedy decoding matched our argmax on 4/4 items. Sampling the first token 400× at temperature 1 put 400/400 draws on a valid option, with empirical frequencies within 0.005–0.040 of our distribution — inside the multinomial noise band of 0.05.

Cross-implementation

Mean W1 computed independently in analyze_run.py reproduces alignment.model from Ted’s code exactly, and matches it to 0.00e+00 over 200 random distribution pairs in the self-test.
