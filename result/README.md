# result/ — LLM rating runs

Everything here is generated by `pipeline/`. See `pipeline/README.md` for how to
reproduce or extend it.

## Layout

```
<model name>/<task>.jsonl                 one line per item: the 7-point PMF,
                                          its expectation, and option_mass
<model name>/<task>.jsonl.meta.json       model, scale, prompt format, the
                                          verbatim first prompt, option token ids
<model name>/<task>.dist.csv              per item: W1, delta, W1/delta, similar
<model name>/<task>.dist.csv.summary.json task-level statistics
logs/                                     SLURM logs
all_runs.csv                              every run in one table (collate.py)
```

Run-name suffixes: `__autofmt` = the standard sweep (the name is historical:
since 2026-09-11 the prompt is the instruction as written, nothing appended),
`__fp32` = scored in float32 rather than bfloat16, `__digits` / `__yesno` =
option set, `__suffix` = an explicit "answer with one number" line appended
(older experiments only).

## Read this before reading any number

**1. Check `option_mass` first.** It is how much probability sat on the valid
answers before renormalising. Below ~0.9 the PMF is a rescaled tail and every
statistic derived from it is meaningless — not "the model disagrees with
humans" but "we did not read the model's answer". Runs known to be invalid for
this reason are kept for the record and marked below.

**2. `alignment.normalized` is not "how human-like".** Its `null` term grows
for models whose distributions are sharp or systematically offset, which
inflates the score. On amouyal it ranks gemma-3-12b second while gemma is last
on the equivalence rate. Prefer `median_W1_over_delta` (1.0 = the model is as
far from the human distribution as one half of the raters is from the other)
and report the raw W1 and equivalence rate alongside.

**3. bfloat16 runs are fine at the task level, not per item.** Expected ratings
correlate 0.997–0.998 with float32, but 15–40% of items have two options with
*exactly* equal probability because bf16 quantises logits in steps of ~0.031.
`__fp32` runs exist for the headline configurations.

## Is the scoring correct?

Yes, verified two ways (`pipeline/verify_scoring.py`, logs in `logs/verify*`):

- **Internal**: in float32, scoring an item alone and inside a padded batch
  agree to 3.3e-05. Left-padding, the attention mask and reading logits at the
  last position are therefore all correct.
- **External**: greedy decoding matched our argmax on 4/4 items, and sampling
  the first token 400× at T=1 put 400/400 draws on a valid option with
  empirical frequencies within 0.005–0.040 of our PMF (multinomial 2sd ≈ 0.05).

As a third check, the mean W1 computed independently in `analyze_run.py`
reproduces `alignment.model` from Ted's code exactly.

### What it shows

- **No model comes close to humans.** The best, Qwen3.5-9B, still sits 1.56×
  the human split-half distance, and at most 2.2% of items pass the equivalence
  test. That holds across 14 models from 0.5B to 32B without exception.
- **Ranking by means and ranking by distributions disagree.** Qwen2.5-32B has
  the best Pearson (0.747) and the third-worst W1/δ (2.37). gemma-3-12b and
  Llama-3.1-8B have near-identical Pearson (0.681 / 0.701) with entropies of
  0.24 and 2.22 bits — one answers almost deterministically, the other is more
  spread out than humans.
- **Scale improves ranking and worsens calibration.** Within Qwen2.5, from
  0.5B to 32B, Pearson climbs 0.040 → 0.383 → 0.473 → 0.601 → 0.719 → 0.747
  while entropy goes 1.42 → 1.61 → 1.93 → 1.20 → 0.81 → 0.62 against a human
  1.77: it rises to roughly human at 3B and then falls monotonically. Bigger
  models order the items better and use the scale less like people do. W1/δ is
  correspondingly non-monotonic — 32B (2.37) is worse than 7B (1.78).
- **On this task the gap is mostly location, not shape — but that does not
  generalise.** Decomposing W1 = |Δmean| + shape (`analyze_run.py`), on amouyal
  the shape term is 2–25%, so 75–98% is per-item mean error. That is *not*
  something a Pearson correlation catches, because correlation is invariant to
  the offset and range compression that produce it (Llama-3.1-8B's OLS slope is
  1.36, Qwen2.5-32B's is 0.85). Within amouyal the shape term tracks
  over-confidence: 21–25% for the sharp models (Qwen2.5-7B/14B/32B,
  gemma-3-12b, entropy 0.23–1.20) against 5–11% for the diffuse ones
  (OLMo-3-7B, Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, entropy 1.93–2.21).

  Across tasks the split moves a lot, tracking how much humans disagree:

  | task | human entropy | shape share |
  |---|--:|---|
  | amouyal2024_plausibility | 1.77 | 2–25% |
  | edwards2024_transparency | 1.91 | 3–15% |
  | lancaster2020_visual | 2.13 | 0–22% |
  | diveica2023_socialness | 2.39 | **27–54%** |

  On diveica, where the human distributions are widest, shape is the majority
  of the gap for Qwen2.5-32B (54%) and close to half for several others. So
  "means are the whole story" is false in general — how much the distributional
  view adds depends on how much the raters disagree. (On the 0/1 dentella task
  shape is exactly 0 by construction: with one cut point W1 *is* the mean
  difference — a useful check that the decomposition is implemented right.)
- **Ranking models by means and by distributions are weakly related.** Kendall
  tau across the 14 models between the Pearson ordering and the W1/δ ordering
  is **+0.385**, and against the equivalence rate **+0.193**
  (`rank_stability.py`). Note the metric that agrees most with Pearson is
  `align` (+0.796) — the one with the null-inflation problem, i.e. the one that
  adds least beyond a mean-level analysis.
- **Which items a model fails on is a property of the model, not the items.**
  Pairwise Spearman of per-item W1 across the 14 models has a median of only
  0.230 (range −0.51 to +0.76): there is no shared set of "hard" sentences.
  What does predict it is the model's own sharpness — across models, the
  correlation between median model entropy and ρ(per-item W1, per-item human
  entropy) is **−0.78**. Models sharper than humans (gemma-3-12b, ρ = +0.52)
  do relatively worse exactly where humans disagree; models flatter than humans
  (Llama-3.2-1B, ρ = −0.46) do relatively better there. Distributional
  alignment is therefore a calibration axis, not something better judgment
  alone would fix. (Computed on the bf16 runs; the per-item bf16 noise averages
  out over 853 items.)
- **Scale-usage pathologies are model-specific and systematic**, not noise.
  Llama-3.1-8B never makes 3 or 7 its mode across 853 items; OLMo-3-7B puts
  0.064 of its mass on {6,7} where humans put 0.500; Qwen2.5-0.5B answers "6"
  for 843 of 853 items.

- **Qwen3.5-9B is the most consistent** (1.35–1.65 across four tasks);
  Llama-3.1-8B has the single best cell (1.01 on lancaster) but the widest
  spread (1.01–2.40).
- **Model rankings are not stable across tasks.** Kendall tau between task
  orderings, on the 6 shared models: diveica/lancaster +0.733,
  amouyal/edwards +0.467, amouyal/diveica +0.333, diveica/edwards +0.333,
  edwards/lancaster +0.333, **amouyal/lancaster +0.067**. Picking a model on
  one task tells you little about another.
- **Task difficulty exceeds model differences.** No model reaches Pearson 0.60
  on lancaster while the same models reach 0.56–0.83 on diveica.
- **Sharpness matching explains the wins.** Llama-3.1-8B, the most diffuse
  model (entropy 2.21–2.32), takes first place on exactly the two tasks with
  the widest human distributions (lancaster human entropy 2.13, diveica 2.39)
  and is beaten on edwards, the narrowest (1.91). gemma-3-12b, the sharpest
  (0.07–0.29 across tasks), is last or near-last everywhere.

## dentella2023_grammaticality — does the option set matter?

The task asks a yes/no question but the repo instruction says "Answer 1 if true
and 0 if false". Scoring the words participants actually saw instead of the
digits (`--option-labels "No,Yes"`):

| model | options | mass | pearson | W1/δ | align |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Meta-Llama-3.1-8B | 0/1 | 0.82 | 0.545 | 5.14 | 0.140 |
| Meta-Llama-3.1-8B | **No/Yes** | **1.00** | **0.566** | **4.19** | **0.286** |
| Qwen2.5-7B | 0/1 | 1.00 | 0.528 | 5.68 | 0.270 |
| Qwen2.5-7B | No/Yes | 1.00 | 0.468 | 4.94 | 0.289 |

It is model-dependent: Llama leaks 18% of its mass elsewhere when forced to
answer with digits and improves on every metric with words, while Qwen is fine
either way and loses a little Pearson. Probe per model rather than assuming.

Note dentella's W1/δ values (4–6) are the worst in the repo, but that is the
denominator: 100 raters per item on a binary scale gives δ = 0.060, so the
ratio inflates. Read it next to the raw W1 (0.21–0.40).
